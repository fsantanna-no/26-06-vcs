## Discussion: does BEC propose anything permissionless?

- Yes, for I-confluent operations only
    - "A system that tolerates arbitrary numbers of
      Byzantine-faulty nodes is immune to Sybil attacks ... being
      immune to Sybil attacks means neither proof-of-work nor the
      central control of permissioned blockchains is required"
- For everything else it defers to Byzantine consensus
    - "a Byzantine consensus algorithm need only be used for those
      transactions that are not I-confluent"
    - offers only PoW ("extraordinarily expensive") or permissioned
      agreement ("permissioned blockchains must control membership
      ... at most f nodes are Byzantine-faulty")

## I-confluence in one example

- Bailis et al. 2014/2015: an operation is invariant-confluent if
  applying it on diverged replicas and merging never violates the
  invariant -> no coordination needed
- Yes: "add a signed post"; union keeps "all posts are signed"
- No: "spend 1 rep" with "balance >= 0"; Alice has 1 rep, likes X
  on replica A and Y on replica B; merge gives -1 (Table 1
  Expense rule; Bitcoin double spend)

## SPAM: the conventionally ignored gap

- BEC full text has zero hits for spam / flood / abuse /
  rate-limit / storage bound
- Correctness model: every correct replica eventually delivers
  every valid update -> honest nodes must absorb anything one
  adversary emits
- "post" is I-confluent -> a single malicious node grows the
  state without bound; BEC forum is correct and unusable at once
- Not a flaw in the theorem, a scope choice that "immune"
  oversells; Douceur framed Sybil as identity/redundancy, not
  resources, and the CRDT/BFT-CRDT line inherited that framing
- Freechains: posting costs reps -> "may this author post now?"
  becomes non-I-confluent -> admission itself is under consensus

## Kleppmann systems are permissioned in practice

- Automerge / local-first apps: invited collaborators
- Keyhive / Beehive (2024-25): capability-based access control is
  the contribution; 2025 PaPoC keynote pairs "BEC" with
  "Local-First Access Control"
- Bluesky/AT: open to read, but signup controlled by the PDS
  operator (invite codes at launch), moderation via labelers
- BEC / BFT-CRDT papers: permissionless in the model, but the
  motivating apps are closed groups
- Pattern: integrity is permissionless, membership is not; the
  admission problem is solved by closing the group
- Contrast for Related Work: two ways to stop one node from
  flooding everyone -- permissioning (Kleppmann) or
  scarce-resource admission (Freechains); only the second keeps
  the network open
