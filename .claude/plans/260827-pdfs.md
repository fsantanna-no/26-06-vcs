## SPAM search (spam|flood|abus|unbounded|junk|DoS|rate-limit)

- `p2p.merkle-crdts` (Sanjuan): ZERO hits; only
  "CRDTs are useful in non-adversarial scenarios, where the
  participating replicas are known to behave correctly";
  "Merkle-CRDTs build a permanent Merkle-DAG which must be
  stored and is ever-growing"; "The size of the DAG might grow
  larger than acceptable"
  -> best evidence: the Merkle-DAG+CRDT paper itself assumes
  no adversary and admits unbounded growth
- `p2p.byz` (Kleppmann PaPoC'22): "vast majority of CRDT
  algorithms actually do not tolerate Byzantine faults, since
  they assume that all participating nodes correctly follow the
  protocol"; no spam/flood
- `p2p.bec` (Kleppmann & Howard): "immune to Sybil attacks means
  neither proof-of-work nor the central control of permissioned
  blockchains is required"; "A potential issue ... is the
  unbounded growth of storage requirements, since the set M
  grows monotonically"; no spam/flood/abuse
- `fed.matrix` (Jacob): "malicious replicas could forge events
  with a high number of parents"; no spam
- `pubsub.activitypub` (W3C): "Spam is a problem in any network,
  perhaps especially so in federated networks. While no specific
  mechanism for combating spam is provided in ActivityPub, it is
  recommended that servers filter incoming content ... through
  some sort of spam filter" (B.6)
  -> protocol-level admission of the gap, federated
- `p2p.mastodon` (Raman): "unclear how ... wide-area malicious
  activity might be detected (e.g., spam bots)"; "The most
  regularly prohibited activity ... is spam. 76% of instances
  disallow it"
- `p2p.nostr` (Wei & Tyson): "the primary purpose of the
  admission fee is to act as a barrier, allowing only genuine
  users while keeping spammers out"
  -> relays resort to out-of-protocol fees
- `p2p.rep.eigentrust` (Kamvar): "the open and anonymous nature
  of these networks leads to a complete lack of accountability
  for the content a peer puts on the network, opening the door to
  abuses"; "the main problem on today's P2P networks is not the
  distribution of malicious executables ... but rather the
  flooding of the network with inauthentic files"
- `pubsub.gossipsub2` (Vyzovitis): "Sybil Attack: This is the
  most common form of attack in P2P networks, since creating
  large numbers of identities is generally cheap resource-wise,
  unless cryptographic puzzles are included"
- `blockchain.wild` (Cachin): "susceptible to denial-of-service
  attacks from clients broadcasting excessively many invalid
  transactions"
- `p2p.sybil` (Douceur): "in the absence of resource
  constraints, denial-of-service attacks are not possible" (model
  assumption; identity, not content)
- `p2p.survey` (2004): "people tend to abuse shared resources
  that they do not have to pay for" (Tragedy of the Commons,
  reputation section)

## Conclusion

- No cited CRDT/DAG paper states "insufficient for SPAM"; they
  either assume no adversary (`p2p.merkle-crdts`) or cover only
  integrity (`p2p.byz`, `p2p.bec`, `fed.matrix`)
- The explicit "no mechanism for spam" admissions come from the
  federated side (`pubsub.activitypub` B.6, `p2p.mastodon`) and
  from measurements (`p2p.nostr` fees)
- Suggested cite for L359 ("nao resolve abuso, spam"):
  `p2p.merkle-crdts` (non-adversarial) + `p2p.byz` (integrity
  only) + `pubsub.activitypub` (spam left to servers) +
  `p2p.rep.eigentrust` (flooding is THE P2P problem)
